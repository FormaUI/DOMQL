# DOMQL Implementation Plan

This plan records the steps that take DOMQL from what is built to what the [specification](domql-specification.md) and the [design](domql-design.md) describe, the decisions that shape them, the state of each step, and where the work departs from the design. The specification is authoritative for the language, and the design for the runtime and its API; this plan changes with the work and is retired when every step is done.

## Built

Each item is what exists and what checks it.

- **The language.** Parsing text and creating queries from their definition, with the position of every mistake, and writing a definition back as text, on one line or laid out as `domql.format` gives a request's canonical text. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition and of writing it.
- **Navigation and absence.** Access that requires a receiver that cannot be null, `?.`, optional-access groups, the coalescing operator and access by name, each recorded in the definition and written back as canonical text, a watch following what each evaluates. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition, of resolution, of evaluation, of watches and of comparing snapshots.
- **Boolean expressions.** `not`, `and` and `or` of Booleans that cannot be null, and conditions of one, each refusing a Boolean that can be null with the fallback to write, a watch following the operands and clauses evaluation reaches; tests are Boolean members, and `is` and `is not` compare strings, numbers, Booleans and nodes, refusing two types that fit neither way. Checked by the tests of parsing, of resolution, of evaluation, of writing, of watches and of modules.
- **Nodes, shadow trees and casts.** `node` and its kinds, each a binding can hold, with `parent`, `root`, `host`, `children`, `first` and `all` on every node, a member on a kind of the name of one on `node` refused; an element's `shadow`, whose `root` and `elements` cross into the tree it hosts and the elements a slot shows; and `as`, which casts to `node` and its kinds and refuses a type that is no node and a target that can never be of it. Checked by the tests of types, of parsing, of the definition, of resolution, of evaluation, of writing and of registration, and in Chromium by what the tree's members and the shadow view answer and observe as nodes move, slots are assigned and shadow roots attached.
- **The vocabulary.** The built-in vocabulary declared as module data, and modules that extend it. Checked by the tests of modules, the registry and the vocabulary.
- **Bindings and resolution.** Every binding typed from its value, and a definition resolved against the vocabulary and its result typed, without a browser; the resolution is kept for its definition, so the queries made from one text share it. Checked by the resolver tests and the tests of `query.resolve`.
- **The query object.** `domql.query` makes a query object from text or a definition, `with` binds it anew and `select` takes a part of its result, and every operation is carried out through it, one the request's kind does not take refused before anything runs. Checked by the tests of the query object, of selections and of refusals.
- **Evaluating.** `evaluate` evaluates every built-in member except `intersects`, which is maintained, and fails it with an evaluation error that says so. `evaluateAsync` waits for the first sample of a maintained member, evaluates again as samples arrive and as what the query depends on changes, and lets go of its observations when it answers, fails or is canceled. Each evaluation records its dependencies, and answers detached, immutable data. Checked by the evaluator tests, in a simulated DOM, and by the browser tests, in Chromium, for geometry, for state the browser decides and for the evaluations that wait.
- **Observations.** The built-in vocabulary names the observations that cover its members' changes, as types a module declares, and observations are started, shared and ended through sessions, following a receiver through shadow trees. There is no public API for them. Checked by the tests of declarations, of the sessions and of the browser observers, and in Chromium by a test of each type of observation reporting a change.
- **Watching.** `query.watch` evaluates a query and evaluates it again when what the result depends on changes, at the next animation frame or in the task that reported the change, raising a `change` event for a result that differs, sharing the branches of the last that did not, and an `error` event for a handler that fails. A watch follows the document, keeps its dependencies through a failed evaluation, recovers, is refreshed and disposed through its handle, and shares its evaluation with its selected views; a late listener receives its baseline first, and a signal ends it. Checked by the tests of watches, of their events and views, and of comparing snapshots, in a simulated DOM, and in Chromium by a watch of a size and of a maintained member.
- **Subscribing.** `query.subscribe` answers an event listener, which starts a subscription's event source in the call and, at each event, captures the fields its event type declares and evaluates the object expression that follows the source, inside the source's delivery, handing its handler the immutable result, or the failure of capturing or projecting it. A maintained member answers its latest sample, or null while it is pending, and is never waited for; the event listener keeps the sessions its last successful projection read, keeps them through a projection that fails, and lets go of everything when disposed. Checked by the tests of projection and of event listeners, in a simulated DOM, and in Chromium by an event listener of clicks and of a maintained member across events.
- **Actions and behaviors.** `executeAsync` executes an action once, after evaluating its receiver and arguments, and answers a promise of its result, checked against the type the action declares and read through the object expression that follows it; a signal cancels it, and nothing an action changed is undone. `apply` puts a behavior into effect in the call and answers a `DomqlBehavior`, whose `update` replaces its bindings whole, validating them before anything changes, whose `error` events carry the failures it reports while it runs, and whose `dispose` ends it once. Checked by the tests of resolving actions and behaviors, of executing actions, and of behaviors, in a simulated DOM, and by the README's examples.
- **Live state.** A watch with `updateStrategy: 'liveState'` keeps its result as one object, updated in place by the change sets between its snapshots, so the root and every object and list beneath it keep their identity, and an item of a list projected from elements keeps its object as it moves. Its `change` event carries the object, the snapshot it was brought to and the change set just applied; a failed evaluation leaves the object as it was. Checked by the tests of applying change sets in place, of live state, and of watches that keep it, in a simulated DOM, and by the README's example.
- **Change sets.** The JSON Patch change sets that bring live state up to date, computed between snapshots; lists projected from elements change by element, through identities kept beside the result. Checked by the tests of computing change sets and of applying them in place, each against expected patches and results and together, and of watches that keep live state.
- **The package.** The bundle, exporting `domql` and `DomqlError` and answering the package's version, with its TypeScript declarations checked against `domql` and against a TypeScript caller; the READMEs' examples run as tests.
- **Measured performance.** Reads and watches made cheaper where a measurement showed the work, every result, error, error location and change set as it was: each node's resolution found by the node and each resolved definition compiled once, each node into a function, with an object expression's field names and a structure's field types found once; a result copied in one pass, and compared with the one before it without copying what did not change; an element's box read once, from its client rects; and a watch holding one session for each observation it reaches, carrying its sessions from each evaluation to the next, comparing only the arguments that can vary, and recording what it depends on only where its caller asks. Measured once arguments that cannot vary went uncompared, against the commit before the work that holds one session per observation, a watch of shaped rows in Chromium reads 22 to 30 percent faster, starts and refreshes 34 to 48 percent faster, disposes 39 to 58 percent faster and holds a third of the script memory, at 1,000 to 100,000 rows, before comparing snapshots without copying made its refresh 4 to 14 percent faster again; and a windowed list of 60 items a virtualizer draws refreshes in 0.22 to 0.43 ms in the simulated DOM, its change sets carrying only what moved, so a list a virtualizer draws needs no machinery of its own. Tried and not kept, for no gain beyond the noise or a loss: freezing the objects and lists an evaluation built where they stand rather than copying them, a rectangle's origin shared together with a structure's fields checked without a function made for each check, and finding a cached session through its observation rather than its content. Measured outside the gate: `npm run bench` from `tests/` compares commits in the simulated DOM the tests use, each workload building its rows at its first iteration, which is a warm-up, so it has the document to itself, at 100 to 100,000 rows, with a windowed list and the change set of 1,000 items in six orders; `npm run scale` times a watch of 1,000 to 100,000 shaped rows in Chromium phase by phase, its read, its start, its disposal and its refresh unchanged, with one row changed and with every row changed under each update strategy, each phase's median, least and greatest time and its cost a row, with the script memory the watch holds. The simulated DOM lays nothing out and keeps a window's mutation observers in one list, so it compares commits and never shows how a cost scales; a change is kept only for a gain repeatable beyond its workload's noise, with no repeatable loss on another.

What is not built: editor tooling and the C# half.

## Decisions

Each decision is open until its state says otherwise, and a step that needs it does not start before. A decision that is built leaves the plan once the specification or the design states what it decided; one a remaining step builds on stays, with its state.

### Accepted

- **D4. What DOMQL owns and what a host owns.** Accepted, and stated by the design; step 15 holds the C# half to it.
- **D12. The API.** Accepted, and stated by the design; its JavaScript half is built, and step 15 builds its C# half.

### Open

- **A behavior that waits.** A behavior that needs to wait before it is in effect would be applied by an `applyAsync` of its own; it is a possibility for later, no part of D12's API, and decided when one is needed.
- **The event sources a behavior offers.** The specification subscribes to them as subscriptions of their own; how a behavior offers one is decided once a behavior that offers one exists.
- **A subscription delivered by frame.** A subscription projects every event in the task it is dispatched in, and a watch already evaluates once a frame however many observations fire; a subscription to events that describe state, as a scroll position does, could take a `schedule: 'frame'` of its own, delivering only the latest projection of each frame and saying that the others are dropped; it would settle whether an event it drops is projected at all, since a projection reads the document as its event is dispatched. The built-in scroll and resize events arrive at most once a frame already, as HTML fires them in its rendering steps, and the others are discrete, so it waits for a source or a query that needs it; waiting for events to go quiet is the caller's policy, carried out in its handler or an extension, and no part of it.
- **A flat-tree parent.** A parent across slots, beside the tree's own `parent` and `root`, waits until its uses and its meaning are clear.
- **An infix call's receiver null policy.** An infix call propagates a null receiver; a policy declared as a parameter's is, accepting null with a meaning of its own or refusing a nullable receiver when the query is prepared, is designed when a member needs one, since no built-in infix call does.
- **What a box observes.** A watch of `rect` misses a move whose cause it does not observe, measured in Chromium: a sibling before it growing, and its container gaining padding, leave the box it reported stale until something else evaluates the watch, while a sibling inserted before it is seen through the structure its ancestry follows. The specification and the declaration state that miss. Whether `rect` should also observe the mutations of the trees it is in, which would catch a move a change to the document causes and still miss one a resource loading or an animation causes, at the cost of evaluating a watch of a box again at every change in its tree, is decided once that cost is measured.
- **What a client size observes.** `clientSize` observes its border box, so a watch misses a change to its padding box that leaves the border box as it was, measured in Chromium for a border and padding changing together under `box-sizing: border-box` and for a border changing alone; a scrollbar appearing where scrollbars take space is missed too, as `clientWidth` is defined, and no test shows it, since headless Chromium's scrollbars take none. Observing its content box as well would catch the scrollbar and the border alone, and still miss a border and padding changing by the same amount, so `clientSize` would stay partly observable; whether that coverage is worth a second observation of each element watched is decided once a query needs it.
- **A popover's open state.** `matches`, `closest`, `first` and `all` observe attributes, structure and focus, and showing or hiding a popover changes none of them, so a watch of `:popover-open` misses it, as their declarations say; observing the `toggle` events of the trees a selector reads would cover it, and waits for a query that needs it.
- **Saying which declaration a value means.** Where a null receiver or an empty list fits declarations that the specification refuses as ambiguous, a caller has no way to say which it means. A way is designed for the first case that needs one, from that case.
- **Ranges of a list, and rebinding a watch.** A `slice` of a list, a general operation whose indexing, bounds and observation would be specified with it, and updating a running watch's bindings, which would settle whether bindings replace the set, what a failed resolution leaves running, how an update meets an evaluation in progress and which observations and live state survive, are two decisions, each made when a query needs it; a window the viewport decides waits for a need and a measured gain.
- **Sharing native observers.** One `ResizeObserver` and one `MutationObserver` of a window serving every element observed alone, each record routed by its target, was prototyped against one observer for each element: in Chromium a watch of 100,000 shaped rows starts 10 to 19 percent faster, refreshes as fast, and disposes 60 to 70 percent slower, each element unobserved on its own, and its script memory is unchanged, since native observers live outside it; start-up is the gain shown so far, and its native memory and how it delivers notifications are not yet measured. It is accepted only with correct routing and independent disposal, so that ending one observation leaves every other working; whether it is built waits for its native memory and its delivery measured and for disposal that does not slow.
- **A list's change set by its longest increasing subsequence.** It would send one operation to move the first item to the end, where the current diff sends one for every other item; the current diff takes at most half a millisecond for 1,000 items in any order, and its `indexOf` and `splice` can grow quadratically with a large reorder. It is decided by the total cost of generating, delivering and applying a change set, once a workload measures delivering and applying them, never by generating one alone; it settles which operations are sent, their order, ties and repeated elements, and the key of a call's bindings computed once waits with it.
- **Incremental evaluation.** Explore incremental evaluation for attribute projections over child elements, including dependency invalidation, retained evaluation state, and fallback to full evaluation. The open question is whether the evaluations that remain valid after a change can be determined reliably: locals, branches and short-circuiting make dependencies conditional, and selectors change membership and order. Measured on 200 rows, five projections and nine changes, re-evaluating an item only where one of its own dependencies fired saves nothing, since `matches`, `all`, `closest` and the ancestry `rect` follows observe a whole tree, so one row's class fires them for every row. Narrowing an observation of a tree to a record on the item, inside it or above it re-evaluates the one row an edit of its own attribute or class changed, and misses a row a selector reads through a sibling, as `tr.selected + tr` does, so it is sound only with each selector analysed for what it reads. Layout reuses no item: a row that grows moves every row after it, none of whose own observations fire, and inserting, removing, moving and scrolling invalidate every row through the structure and the scrolling they observe. What remains is a large saving for edits of a row's own attributes and class, and none for the changes lists most often see, so incremental evaluation waits for a workload that needs that saving.

### D14. A syntax tree for tooling

Accepted; built by step 14. One error-tolerant parser builds a lossless syntax tree of the text, its tokens and their trivia, comments among them, and the definition is lowered from that tree, so there is one grammar and one parser, and the definition stays the only contract the runtime reads and evaluates. The tree is internal until step 14's tooling API exposes it.

- **Nodes for constructs.** A node stands for a construct written, named after the grammar rule that produces it, as `equality`, `conjunction` and `member-access-expression` are; a rule that only passes an operand on, a level of precedence or a repetition, produces no node of its own, so `@panel` holds no wrapper for each level it passes through. Tokens and trivia carry the complete text, so the tree gives the text back character for character.
- **Recovery for the editor alone.** The parser records a missing token and skips an unexpected one, so a text being written gives a tree and multiple useful diagnostics without excessive cascades. Creating a query from text still refuses any syntax error; a recovered tree is read by tooling, and a missing token is never taken as permission to guess what the text meant.
- **From syntax to meaning.** Lowering records the syntax node each definition node comes from, and a resolution maps each definition node to its declarations and types, so tooling joins a position in the text to what resolution knows of it: completion after `@panel.` reads the receiver's type, and renaming a local follows the scope resolution records for it and refuses a name another binding would capture.
- **Formatting.** A query keeps no syntax tree, so the runtime's memory is as it is, and `domql.format` keeps its meaning, the canonical text of a request, which no comment survives. Formatting that preserves comments is a tooling operation over a document's syntax tree.
- **Measured before it is committed:** uncached parsing and lowering, their allocations, the memory a tree retains and the bundle's size, against the current parser, on valid input and on malformed input, an editor's usual text; a watch's refresh, which never touches a syntax tree, stays as it is.
- **Deferred until a need is concrete:** incremental reparsing, specialized tree machinery, and a public tree schema shared by C# and JavaScript.

## Steps

A step is done when its tests pass in the gate, its documents say what it built, and its state here says so. Completed steps leave the plan. Built describes the capabilities they delivered.

The remaining work is the beta, then editor tooling and the C# half, which follow it.

### 22. Beta

Make the language and the JavaScript API ready for external use: no breaking redesign is planned, the limitations that remain are documented, and a breaking change after it is exceptional, recorded and given migration guidance.

- **Needs:** nothing beyond what is built.
- **Done when:**
  - a cast inside an optional-access group is settled: which compositions the grammar allows and the parser refuses, as `(?((@a ?? @b) as "element").rect)` and `(?((?@a.b) as "element").c)`, and why, are established, and the parser reads each one the grammar allows whose meaning is clear, the grammar staying as it is until that is known;
  - the module API is audited as the documents were, what `createModule`, `registerModule`, a declaration and the functions it names accept checked against what the design and the declarations say, each finding fixed or documented as a limitation. Known so far: what an observation's `sample` answers is not checked, so one answering a bare value reads as a sample that is never pending; `query.bindings` is public and typed, and whether it belongs to the API is decided; and a class instance a module answers for a structured type, as a `DOMRect` is, reads its fields through access and is copied as an empty object where the result holds it whole;
  - `CHANGELOG.md` exists, empty until the first change after the beta, from which it records each change a caller must act on, with how to migrate; nothing changed before the beta is recorded, since no caller depends on it;
  - the specification's version is `1.0.0-beta`, the package's `1.0.0-beta.N`, numbered by its commits as the alpha is, and a run from the sources reports `1.0.0-beta+source`, wherever a version is written or checked: the package project, the specification's and the design's titles, `Specification.mjs`, `domql.js`, the declarations, the agent guide's version rules, the CI workflow's comments, and the alignment and bundle tests.
- **State:** the specification, the design, the READMEs and the guides are audited and corrected; the cast, the module API audit, the changelog and the version are not started.

### 14. Editor tooling

Diagnostics and completion over the grammar and the declaration metadata, without running a capability.

- **Needs:** D14, and nothing of the runtime.
- **Done when:** a query text is checked against the declarations available to its project, reporting the same positions and messages as resolution, through the syntax tree D14 decides.
- **State:** not started.

### 15. The C# half

The query object of D12 for a .NET host: `Domql.Query(text, bindings)` and its operations, `Domql.TryQuery`, typed mapping and snapshots, with `CancellationToken` for cancellation.

- **Needs:** D4 and D12, whose JavaScript half is built.
- **Keeping a watch's result in C#:** the adapter that carries a watch to the host owns its synchronization, and an application starts a watch and receives its results.
  - It watches live state by default.
  - It takes the initial baseline from the first ready event's `args.snapshot`.
  - It queues the changes after it, which are immutable, and delivers one update at a time, in order, sending the next only once the receiving side acknowledges that the one before it was applied completely. This is a scope decision; batching would not in itself need revisions, since consecutive change sets apply in order.
  - The acknowledgment follows application alone, never the application handlers, and a failed delivery stops every further update of that watch.
  - It applies each update so that no consumer observes a change set partly applied: access to the C# result while an update is applied follows an explicit rule, and a result whose application failed becomes unusable. Application handlers are notified once the update is applied.
  - It rejects a delivery from an obsolete watch on the receiving side, before applying it, through a token of its own for the watch it is following.
  - Where applying an update throws, a call rejects, or a call times out, leaving its outcome uncertain, it first invalidates the old watch, so that watch's late applications and acknowledgments change neither the replacement result nor the new queue, and then disposes it and rebuilds the result from a fresh watch's baseline.
  - Where the connection is lost with the components that hold its results, the components that replace them start watches of their own; where a connection resumes with the existing components, an update unanswered across the interruption is uncertain, and the adapter recovers as from a timeout.
  - It reports an exception from an application handler without restarting synchronization, since the state was already accepted.
  - Its application of change sets passes a conformance corpus the JavaScript patcher passes too: each case a previous result, a change set and the result they give, the expected results reviewed independently of either applier, moves and escaped paths among them.
- **State:** not started.

None. A departure is recorded here as it happens, with the reason, until the design is brought into line.
